What to Know Before Buying a Home in Daybreak, UT

By Tamara Zander, Real Estate Agent, Zander Real Estate Team  ·  Updated October 2026

Daybreak sits within the city of South Jordan, UT, and it's grown into a community of over 43,000 residents since it opened in 2004. Nearly 10,000 homes have been built around a 250-acre man-made freshwater lake - that alone tells you this place operates differently than the rest of Salt Lake County.

Buying here means choosing a specific village, wrapping your head around association fees, and honestly assessing your commute to downtown. This guide walks through the types of homes in Daybreak, current market data, and the steps you'll need to close on a property in this master-planned community.

Current Market Conditions for Daybreak Real Estate

Recent data from the South Jordan, UT market puts the median sale price at roughly $625,000 as of mid-2026, with homes spending about 45 days on the market before going under contract. That's a meaningful shift from the frantic pace buyers dealt with a few years back in the Daybreak housing market.

Sale-to-list ratios are sitting right around 99%, so sellers are still in a decent position - but with 3.7 months of supply and 369 homes sold in the recent reporting period, this isn't a one-sided market. The median dipped slightly year-over-year, and late 2025 data shows prices were hovering around $592,000, which gives you a sense of the trajectory. Pricing within Daybreak itself varies considerably depending on which village you're looking at and how close the property is to Oquirrh Lake.

Is Now a Good Time to Buy?

There are currently 447 available homes in the broader South Jordan area. That 45-day median time on market means you can actually tour a few properties and think before someone snatches them - a real departure from what this market looked like before. Daily inventory tracking still matters, and a good agent will flag new listings as soon as they hit.

Who Usually Buys Here

The buyer pool is a real mix: first-timers, out-of-state relocators, and investors looking at long-term rentals all show up here. First-time buyers tend to gravitate toward townhomes and smaller-footprint properties near the community centers. Relocators usually want the larger single-family homes in established villages with direct trail and water access.

Housing Types and Price Ranges in the Community

The inventory spans entry-level condominiums all the way up to expansive luxury estates, and because the community has been developing in phases for over two decades, you're choosing between older established builds and brand-new construction - sometimes within the same general area.

Architecture changes meaningfully from village to village, so you won't find rows of identical houses. Buyers watching their budget can look at the attached housing segments, which also come with less exterior maintenance. Those targeting premium properties tend to focus on homes along the lake or with mountain views.

Single-Family and New Construction Options

Single-family homes make up the bulk of the Daybreak market, ranging from modest cottages to large custom builds. New construction is a consistent option as the community's footprint keeps expanding - ask your agent about builder timelines and any incentives for using preferred lenders, because those details change frequently.

Condominiums and Townhomes

Townhomes and condos give you a lower entry point and access to all the same amenities without the upkeep of a large yard. They tend to sit closer to commercial centers and transit stops. Just make sure you're factoring the specific monthly association dues for those buildings into your budget from the start - it's an easy line item to underestimate.

Daybreak Villages to Consider

Daybreak is divided into distinct named villages, each with its own architectural style and amenities. Buyers often compare these areas to find the best neighborhoods in Daybreak for their specific needs. Founders, the original village, got things started in 2004. Eastlake Village followed in 2006 and has stayed one of the most searched areas in the community - it features some of the largest homes, easy access to Oquirrh Lake, and the Oquirrh Mountain LDS Temple as a prominent local landmark.

North Shore opened in 2008, and the community has since grown to include Highland Park, South Station, Cascade, Watermark, and The Island at Oquirrh Lake. Every village connects to the broader trail system and has its own pocket parks.

Established and Popular Villages

Eastlake and Founders both offer mature landscaping and settled neighborhood layouts. Buyers who want immediate lake access usually zero in on Eastlake or The Island at Oquirrh Lake. Central locations and larger lot sizes have helped those areas hold their value.

Active Adult and 55+ Options

Garden Park and Springhouse are designed specifically for residents 55 and older, with single-level living and specialized community centers. They're quieter by nature, but residents still have full access to the broader master-planned amenities - it's not a trade-off situation.

Location, Commutes, and County Details

Daybreak falls entirely within Salt Lake County and takes up a large portion of the city of South Jordan. When reviewing Daybreak zip codes, the community's primary one is 84009, though nearby South Jordan addresses also use 84095, 84096, 84081, and 84065.

The drive to downtown Salt Lake City is about 22 miles via Highway 85 and typically runs around 20 minutes - workable for most commuters who want suburban space without sacrificing city access. Light rail stations within the community give you an alternative to driving on days when you'd rather not deal with the highway. Getting your arms around these boundaries before you start touring homes will save you from any unpleasant commute surprises later.

Driving Distances and Airport Access

Salt Lake City International Airport is about 17 miles north of the community. You can get there without threading through downtown, which matters more than people expect once they're doing it regularly.

Cost of Living and Local Lifestyle

The average household income in this part of South Jordan is about $145,000, and to comfortably carry a median-priced home at around $625,000, buyers generally need to earn roughly $126,000 annually. That's a useful benchmark, but your actual number depends on what you're putting down and what rates are doing when you close.

Daily life here centers on Oquirrh Lake - the 250-acre man-made freshwater feature that the whole community was designed around. Paddleboarding, kayaking, and fishing are the warm-weather staples. The trail network connecting the villages is extensive enough that you can handle a lot of local errands on foot or by bike without touching your car. Utah's four seasons are real - hot summers, snowy winters - and the community's indoor recreation centers handle the months when the parks are less appealing. Understanding these local amenities helps when weighing the pros and cons of living in Daybreak.

Income Requirements and Affordability

The $126,000 figure is the estimated income needed for a median purchase. If you're targeting condos or smaller townhomes, your entry point comes down. If you're looking at large single-family homes in Eastlake, budget significantly higher. A lender conversation early in your search - before you fall in love with a specific property - is worth the hour.

Working With a Local Real Estate Agent

Not every South Jordan agent has real fluency with Daybreak's village structures, HOA rules, and builder timelines. That gap matters more here than it would in a standard subdivision, so it's worth asking specifically about 84009 experience when you're interviewing candidates to find the best real estate agent in Daybreak.

A knowledgeable agent will walk you through how older resales in Founders compare to new construction in the expanding villages, explain how the community enhancement fees work, and tell you exactly what the monthly association dues cover. That kind of detail keeps the closing process from producing unwelcome surprises. Interview a few people, see who communicates clearly, and look at their track record in Salt Lake County.

Choosing the Right Representation

Your agent should be able to explain the real differences between Highland Park, North Shore, and Watermark without pausing to look anything up. They should know which areas offer the best light rail access and which sit closest to the lake. Relationships with local lenders who understand the area's condo financing requirements don't hurt either.

Understanding Agent Fees

In Utah, seller and buyer agent commissions are negotiated at the start of the listing or representation agreement. Ask your agent to explain how they're compensated before you sign a buyer broker agreement. It's a straightforward conversation, and any agent worth working with will have it without making it awkward.

Steps to Purchase Your Utah Home

Start with a pre-approval letter from a licensed lender. It tells sellers you're serious and establishes exactly how much you can borrow - without it, you're touring homes in the dark.

Once you're pre-approved, you and your agent will tour properties focused on the villages that actually fit your budget and lifestyle. When you find the right place, your agent drafts an offer using recent comparable sales from that specific village. Seller accepts, and you're into the due diligence and inspection period.

Hire a licensed inspector regardless of whether the home is a resale or brand new. New construction can have builder oversights that won't surface until someone looks specifically for them.

Securing Pre-Approval

A strong pre-approval accounts for Salt Lake County property taxes and the community's HOA fees - not just your base mortgage. Lenders will review your income, debt-to-income ratio, and credit score to set your loan limits. Having that groundwork done means you can move quickly when a lakefront property hits the market, which is exactly when speed matters.

Managing Closing Costs and Inspections

Budget for standard Utah closing costs: title insurance, loan origination fees, and appraisal costs. On top of those, this master-planned community has specific transfer or enhancement fees the association requires at closing. Your agent and lender will give you a detailed estimate well before your closing date - you shouldn't be seeing those numbers for the first time at the closing table.

Frequently Asked Questions About Daybreak Real Estate

How much are the HOA fees when buying a home in Daybreak, UT, and what exactly do they include?

It depends on the property type and village. Condominiums and townhomes carry different fee structures than single-family homes in Eastlake or Founders. Verify the exact monthly dues and what they cover with your agent before you make an offer - don't assume.

What is the Daybreak Community Enhancement Fee and does the buyer or seller pay it at closing?

The community enhancement fee is a transfer fee the association requires when a property changes hands. Who pays it gets negotiated during the offer process, which is an important detail when selling your home in Daybreak. Your agent can help you structure the contract so there's no ambiguity about who's covering it.

What are the main differences between the various villages when looking for a home in Daybreak?

Age, architecture, and amenities. Founders is the original 2004 village. Eastlake has some of the largest homes in the community and direct Oquirrh Lake access. Garden Park is a dedicated 55+ community. Each village has its own location and character within the broader South Jordan area.

How long is the current waitlist and build timeline if I want to buy new construction in Daybreak?

It depends on the specific developer and which village you're buying in. The community has been expanding since 2004 with nearly 10,000 homes built, so new phases open on a rolling basis. Check directly with builders or ask your local agent for current construction schedules - those timelines shift.

Are there strict HOA rules, exterior restrictions, or parking limits I need to know about before buying a house in Daybreak?

Yes. The association maintains specific rules governing exterior modifications and property upkeep across the community. Those guidelines are what keep the architectural variety in villages like North Shore and Highland Park intact. Read all association documents during your due diligence period - don't skim them.

Do properties in Daybreak, UT qualify for zero-down USDA loans or other Utah-specific first-time buyer grants?

It depends on the specific program's geographic and income requirements. Because Daybreak is within the city of South Jordan in Salt Lake County, it may not meet the rural location criteria for a USDA loan. Talk to a local lender about which Utah housing grants and financing options actually apply to your situation.
