Daybreak Village Guide: Every Neighborhood Explained

What are the villages and neighborhoods in Daybreak, Utah? Daybreak is organized into three areas: the original Lower Villages around Oquirrh Lake, Downtown Daybreak, and the newer Upper Villages, thirteen neighborhoods in all, including two age-restricted 55+ communities.

People ask us the same question in a dozen different ways: "Which part of Daybreak should I actually live in?" It's the right question, because Daybreak isn't one neighborhood, it's thirteen, spread across three distinct areas that each grew up in a different chapter of the community's story. This guide goes deep on every one so you can walk into a tour already knowing what to expect, not guessing from a map.

What ties all thirteen together is the same underlying design: front porches close enough to invite a conversation, smaller yards backed by bigger shared parks and trails, and almost everything you need within a five-minute walk or bike ride. The details differ by neighborhood. That philosophy doesn't.

The Lower Villages

The Lower Villages are where Daybreak started: the original neighborhoods built around Oquirrh Lake. They've had the most time to grow in, mature trees, settled landscaping, a lived-in feel, but new construction still shows up here too, including a small collection of luxury homes on The Island. If you want a brand-new home inside a neighborhood that's already found its rhythm, this is where to look.

Garden Park (55+ Community)

Garden Park is Daybreak's original age-restricted community, open to residents 55 and older. It's established and settled, with mature landscaping and a front-porch culture where neighbors actually know each other. Homes skew single-family, generally on the smaller, lower-maintenance end, built for buyers who want an active adult lifestyle without stepping away from Daybreak's trails and lake.

Good to know: Age-restricted communities require at least one resident per household to be 55 or older, with HOA rules to match. Ask us for the specific occupancy requirements before you tour.

Highland Park

Highland Park threads the needle between quiet and connected. Streets here are calmer than SoDa Row, but the trail network puts the lake within easy reach for a morning walk or an evening bike ride, so you're never truly cut off from the rest of Daybreak.

The home mix in Highland Park includes both single-family homes and townhomes, which makes it one of the more flexible neighborhoods for buyers who haven't fully decided between the two.

Good to know: Highland Park's trail connections vary by exact location. If lake access matters to you, we'll map the actual walk or bike time from any specific listing before you tour.

↑ Back to comparison table

Eastlake

Eastlake sits closest to Oquirrh Lake itself, and it's the neighborhood to prioritize if water views or daily trail access aren't a nice-to-have, they're the reason you're moving to Daybreak in the first place. Homes here skew single-family, generally with a more premium finish level than the community average.

Good to know: Not every Eastlake address has a direct water view, some are simply lake-adjacent by proximity. Ask us for the specific vantage point before you fall in love with a listing photo.

↑ Back to comparison table

Creekside

Creekside leans toward townhomes and a genuinely lower-maintenance lifestyle, built for buyers who want everything Daybreak offers, trails, the lake, Downtown Daybreak, without a large yard tying up their weekends. Lot sizes here are smaller by design, which typically keeps price points more approachable.

Good to know: Townhome HOA fees in Creekside typically cover more shared maintenance than single-family HOAs. Ask for the fee breakdown so you're comparing true monthly cost, not just the sticker price.

↑ Back to comparison table

Founders Park

Founders Park has one of the widest mixes of home types and price points in Daybreak, which makes it a natural starting point for buyers who are still comparing neighborhoods and don't want to commit to one style yet.

Good to know: Because Founders Park has more variety, price-per-square-foot comparisons matter more here than neighborhood averages. We build that comparison for every Founders Park listing we walk you through.

↑ Back to comparison table

North Shore

North Shore sits along the lake's northern edge, one of the original villages with lake frontage. It offers a similar water-oriented feel to Eastlake, generally at a somewhat more approachable price point, since it's had longer to establish itself.

Good to know: As with Eastlake, confirm the exact lake orientation of any North Shore listing. Direct-frontage homes and lake-adjacent homes are priced differently.

↑ Back to comparison table

The Island

The Island is exactly what it sounds like: a small collection of luxury homes surrounded by Oquirrh Lake, inside one of Daybreak's most established neighborhoods. It's a rare pairing, brand-new construction with water on every side, in a village that's had decades to mature around it.

Good to know: Inventory on The Island is limited by design, there's only so much island. If this is your target, tell us now so we can flag new listings the moment they're available.

↑ Back to comparison table

Downtown Daybreak

Daybreak was designed from the start to be a complete community, not just a place to live, but a place with everything daily life needs built in. Downtown Daybreak is where that shows up most: restaurants and gathering spots, family entertainment, and easy walking, biking, or light rail access to work. A new ballpark is taking shape here too, planned as the future home of the Salt Lake Bees, with new homes going in around it right now.

SoDa Row

SoDa Row is Daybreak's original town center: coffee shops, restaurants, and the community events that give Daybreak its social calendar, all within a short walk of nearby homes. Homes closest to SoDa Row lean toward townhomes and condos, with single-family homes further out.

Good to know: Homes directly on or facing SoDa Row can carry a premium for the walkability. Ask us about comparable homes one or two streets back if you want the lifestyle at a lower price point.

↑ Back to comparison table

Center Field Row

Center Field Row is Daybreak's newest downtown expansion, taking shape around the planned ballpark that's set to become the new home of the Salt Lake Bees. Expect new dining and entertainment options, easy light rail access, and new construction homes as the district fills in.

Good to know: Because Center Field Row is still developing, amenities and construction timelines shift. We track this district closely and can give you the current status before you tour.

↑ Back to comparison table

The Upper Villages

The Upper Villages are Daybreak's newest chapter, built with everything the community has learned since the Lower Villages broke ground. New home designs, new parks (including a large city park), an expanding network of trails and bike paths, and a standout bike park all live up here. The Upper Villages also have their own answer to Oquirrh Lake: the Watercourse, a connected system of waterways and outdoor space running through Watermark, Cascade, and Springhouse.

Heights Park

Heights Park sits on Daybreak's higher ground, with newer construction and views back toward the valley and lake below. It suits buyers who want a newer build and don't mind trading a bit of lake proximity for elevation and a quieter, less-built-out feel.

Good to know: Because Heights Park is part of the newer expansion, home designs and floor plans here tend to be more current than in the Lower Villages. Ask us to compare floor plans side by side.

↑ Back to comparison table

Watermark

Watermark takes its name seriously: it sits along the Upper Villages' Watercourse, giving buyers here their own version of the water-adjacent lifestyle Eastlake and North Shore offer down in the Lower Villages, in a newer-construction package.

Good to know: Watercourse-adjacent lots carry a premium similar to lake-adjacent lots in the Lower Villages. Ask us for a direct price comparison if water access is a priority.

↑ Back to comparison table

Cascade

Cascade is one of the more family-oriented Upper Villages, close to the new parks and trail connections that define this part of Daybreak. Home types run from single-family to townhomes, generally in newer floor plans than what's available in the Lower Villages.

Good to know: As new phases of Cascade release, pricing and lot availability shift quickly. If you're set on this neighborhood, ask us to flag new releases before they're widely listed.

↑ Back to comparison table

Springhouse (55+ Community)

Springhouse is Daybreak's newest age-restricted community, open to residents 55 and older, built with the same active adult focus as Garden Park but with the newer home designs and amenities that come with the Upper Villages.

Good to know: As with Garden Park, confirm current age-restriction and occupancy rules before you tour, since HOA specifics can differ between the two 55+ communities.

How to Choose

If you've read this far and still can't decide, that's normal, and it's exactly why we don't recommend picking a neighborhood from a map or a blog post alone. We'd rather drive you through two or three that match your budget and lifestyle, and let you feel which one fits.

  • Want to walk everywhere? Start with SoDa Row or Center Field Row.
  • Want the lake as part of daily life? Start with Eastlake, North Shore, or The Island.
  • Want quiet with quick trail access? Start with Highland Park.
  • Want low-maintenance living? Start with Creekside.
  • Want an active 55+ community? Start with Garden Park or Springhouse.
  • Want the newest construction and floor plans? Start with Heights Park, Watermark, or Cascade.
  • Still deciding on budget and style? Start with Founders Park.

Daybreak is compact enough that a single afternoon of touring usually settles the question for good.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many villages and neighborhoods are in Daybreak, Utah?

Daybreak is organized into three areas: the Lower Villages around Oquirrh Lake (Garden Park, Highland Park, Eastlake, Creekside, Founders Park, North Shore, and The Island), Downtown Daybreak (SoDa Row and Center Field Row), and the Upper Villages (Heights Park, Watermark, Cascade, and Springhouse).

Which Daybreak neighborhoods are age-restricted 55+ communities?

Garden Park in the Lower Villages and Springhouse in the Upper Villages are both age-restricted 55+ communities. Springhouse is Daybreak's newest 55+ neighborhood.

What are the Upper Villages in Daybreak?

The Upper Villages are Daybreak's newest expansion: Heights Park, Watermark, Cascade, and Springhouse. They include new home designs, expanded parks and trails, a large bike park, and the Watercourse, a chain of waterways that serves as the Upper Villages' answer to Oquirrh Lake.

What is Downtown Daybreak and Center Field Row?

Downtown Daybreak is the community's dining, entertainment, and transit hub, anchored by SoDa Row and the newer Center Field Row district, which is taking shape around a planned ballpark intended to become the new home of the Salt Lake Bees.

Which Daybreak village is closest to Oquirrh Lake?

Several Lower Villages border the lake directly, and The Island sits surrounded by it. Eastlake and North Shore also offer quick lake and trail access. We can walk you through current lake-adjacent listings by village when you call.

Is one Daybreak village more affordable than the others?

Pricing shifts by home type and lot more than by village label. Founders Park and Creekside often include more attached and starter-friendly options, but every village has a range. We pull current comps by village and price point when you call.
