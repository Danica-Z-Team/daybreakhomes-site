Daybreak Village Guide: Which Village Fits You

What are the villages in Daybreak, Utah? Daybreak, South Jordan is organized into several villages, including Garden Park, SoDa Row, Highland Park, Eastlake, Creekside, and Founders Park, each with its own streetscape, home mix, and distance from the lake and trails.

People ask us the same question in a dozen different ways: "Which part of Daybreak should I actually live in?" It's the right question, because Daybreak isn't one neighborhood, it's six or more, each with its own personality. This guide goes deep on every village so you can walk into a tour already knowing what to expect, not guessing from a map.

Garden Park

Garden Park is one of Daybreak's more established villages, and it shows in the best way: mature landscaping, a settled rhythm, and a front-porch culture where you actually recognize your neighbors' cars in the driveway. Streets here tend to feel less like a new development and more like a neighborhood that's already found its footing.

Homes in Garden Park skew single-family, with a mix of architectural styles from craftsman-influenced to more traditional facades. Lot sizes vary block to block, so it's worth touring a few streets rather than judging the village from one listing photo.

Garden Park sits far enough from SoDa Row and the lake that you'll typically drive or bike rather than walk, but close enough that neither is ever more than a few minutes away. It tends to suit buyers who want a quieter daily rhythm without giving up access to the rest of the community.

Good to know: Because Garden Park has been established longer, some homes have already been through one round of updates, ask about renovation history and HOA reserve contributions when you tour.

SoDa Row

SoDa Row is Daybreak's town center: coffee shops, restaurants, and the community events that give Daybreak its social calendar, all within a short walk of nearby homes. If your ideal weekend involves walking to breakfast rather than driving to it, this is the village to prioritize.

Homes closest to SoDa Row lean toward townhomes and condos, with single-family homes further out as you move away from the retail core. That mix means SoDa Row can work for buyers at a range of budgets, not just luxury condo buyers.

The trade-off for walkability is proximity to activity: expect more foot traffic, more visible community life, and less of the hushed cul-de-sac feel you'd find in Garden Park. For a lot of buyers, that's exactly the draw.

Good to know: Homes directly on or facing SoDa Row can carry a premium for the walkability. Ask us about comparable homes one or two streets back if you want the lifestyle at a lower price point.

Highland Park

Highland Park threads the needle between quiet and connected. Streets here are calmer than SoDa Row, but the trail network puts the lake within easy reach for a morning walk or an evening bike ride, so you're never truly cut off from the rest of Daybreak.

The home mix in Highland Park includes both single-family homes and townhomes, which makes it one of the more flexible villages for buyers who haven't fully decided between the two. Streetscapes tend to be a bit more contemporary than Garden Park's more traditional feel.

Highland Park tends to attract buyers who want a quiet street to come home to, but who'd rather bike to the lake on a Tuesday than plan a special trip for it.

Good to know: Highland Park's trail connections vary by exact location within the village. If lake access matters to you, we'll map the actual walk or bike time from any specific listing before you tour.

Eastlake

Eastlake sits closest to Oquirrh Lake itself, and it's the village to prioritize if water views or daily trail access aren't a nice-to-have, they're the reason you're moving to Daybreak in the first place.

Homes here skew single-family, generally with a more premium finish level than the community average, reflecting the lake-adjacent land. Streetscapes are designed with the lake in mind: more homes oriented toward water or green space, more trail access points nearby.

Because Eastlake is one of the more sought-after villages for lake access, inventory can be tighter here than elsewhere in Daybreak. Buyers serious about this village should be ready to move when the right listing appears.

Good to know: Not every Eastlake address has a direct water view, some are simply lake-adjacent by proximity. Ask us for the specific vantage point before you fall in love with a listing photo.

Creekside

Creekside leans toward townhomes and a genuinely lower-maintenance lifestyle, built for buyers who want everything Daybreak offers, trails, the lake, SoDa Row, without a large yard tying up their weekends.

Lot sizes here are smaller by design, which typically keeps price points more approachable than single-family-heavy villages like Eastlake or Garden Park. That makes Creekside a common landing spot for downsizers and first-time buyers alike.

The village sits within a comfortable bike ride of both the lake and SoDa Row, so "low-maintenance" doesn't mean "cut off." You're trading yard space for convenience, not trading away the lifestyle.

Good to know: Townhome HOA fees in Creekside typically cover more shared maintenance than single-family village HOAs. Ask for the fee breakdown so you're comparing true monthly cost, not just the sticker price.

Founders Park

Founders Park rounds out the community with one of the widest mixes of home types and price points in Daybreak, which makes it a natural starting point for buyers who are still comparing villages and don't want to commit to one style yet.

Because the mix is broad, "the Founders Park feel" varies more block to block than in some other villages. That's not a downside, it just means touring a few different streets matters more here than it does in a more uniform village like Eastlake.

Founders Park tends to suit buyers who care more about value and flexibility than a single defined aesthetic, and buyers who want room to move within the village later without leaving Daybreak entirely.

Good to know: Because Founders Park has more variety, price-per-square-foot comparisons matter more here than village averages. We build that comparison for every Founders Park listing we walk you through.

How to Choose

If you've read this far and still can't decide, that's normal, and it's exactly why we don't recommend picking a village from a map or a blog post alone. We'd rather drive you through two or three villages that match your budget and lifestyle, and let you feel which one fits.

A useful way to narrow it down before you call: rank walkability, quiet, and water access in order of importance to you. That ranking alone usually points you toward two villages worth touring first.

  • Want to walk everywhere? Start with SoDa Row.
  • Want the lake as part of daily life? Start with Eastlake.
  • Want quiet with quick trail access? Start with Highland Park or Garden Park.
  • Want low-maintenance living? Start with Creekside.
  • Still deciding on budget and style? Start with Founders Park.

Daybreak is compact enough that a single afternoon of touring usually settles the question for good.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many villages are in Daybreak, Utah?

Daybreak is organized into several distinct villages, including Garden Park, SoDa Row, Highland Park, Eastlake, Creekside, and Founders Park, each with its own home mix, streetscape, and proximity to the lake and trails.

Which Daybreak village is closest to Oquirrh Lake?

Eastlake sits closest to the lake itself, with Highland Park and parts of SoDa Row also offering quick trail access. We can walk you through current lake-adjacent listings by village when you call.

Which Daybreak village has the most retail and dining?

SoDa Row is Daybreak's main retail and dining hub, with coffee shops, restaurants, and community events within walking distance of nearby homes.

Which Daybreak village is best for low-maintenance living?

Creekside leans toward townhomes and smaller lots, which tends to suit buyers who want the Daybreak lifestyle without a large yard to maintain.

Is one Daybreak village more affordable than the others?

Pricing shifts by home type and lot more than by village label. Founders Park and Creekside often include more attached and starter-friendly options, but every village has a range. We pull current comps by village and price point when you call.
