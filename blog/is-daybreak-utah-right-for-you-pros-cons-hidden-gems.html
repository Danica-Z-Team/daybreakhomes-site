Living in Daybreak

Is Daybreak Right for You? The Honest Pros, Cons, and Hidden Gems of Daybreak, Utah

By Tamara Zander, Real Estate Agent, Zander Real Estate Team  ·  Updated November 2026

Is Daybreak, Utah a good place to live? For people who want trails, parks, water, and community right outside the door, Daybreak in South Jordan (84009) is hard to beat. The trade-offs: small yards, ongoing construction, HOA rules, and commuter traffic.

Tamara Zander moved into Daybreak in December 2004. Her family was the 13th to move in, and everything around their house was dirt. She wasn't even sure she wanted to stay. "I don't know if I'm an HOA gal," she told her husband. They made a deal: if they didn't like it in three years, they'd move.

That was more than 22 years ago. Tamara now serves on the South Jordan City Council, leads the Zander Real Estate Team, and says she'll never leave.

So yes, we're biased. But in this video tour, Tamara covers the parts of Daybreak that might not work for you, too. Here's the full rundown: the pros, the cons, what's coming, and the hidden gem that shapes her life every day.

The Pros: What Daybreak Does Better Than Anywhere Else in Utah

The Cove and the Watercourse

The Cove is a gathering place in Upper Daybreak, and it sets the tone for the whole community. Ping-pong tables and cornhole stay out year round, and residents can check out paddles and bags for free. Inside, there's a fireplace lounge any resident can use, and there's a Swig right there when you need a drink.

Outside, the Watercourse winds past fire pits, bridges, and a small beach. From Memorial Day to Labor Day, residents show their Daybreak card at the brightly painted paddle board shack and check out a board for free. You can also launch your own kayak, canoe, or paddle board.

Paddle about half a mile down the Watercourse, under two bridges, and you'll reach the paddle board house, where residents can keep their board in a locker. No hauling it from home.

"I didn't have to drive 45 minutes to the mountains to find a reservoir," Tamara says. "I've got it in my backyard."

Oquirrh Lake: The Crown Jewel

Oquirrh Lake is the heart of Daybreak, circled by trails full of walkers, bikers, scooters, strollers, and rollerbladers, with footbridges out to the island. You'll see kids and adults fishing here (mostly catch and release).

Tamara has a personal connection to this one. When the first phase of the lake was finished, she checked all four of her kids out of school to watch a tanker truck pump fish into the water. "This is history," she told a reporter on the shore that day. Twenty years later, the lake is thriving.

Over 50 Miles of Trails

Daybreak has more than 50 miles of trails inside the community. They connect every village to the lake, so you can get almost anywhere on foot or by bike without fighting traffic.

Across from Highland Park, the Spoke Bike Park is a professionally designed mountain bike trail system built right into the neighborhood, with routes from beginner to advanced. "I got on these trails with my e-bike and they're actually quite difficult," Tamara admits.

Parks That Are Each Designed Differently

Daybreak's parks aren't copy-and-paste. Some are small pocket parks tucked into neighborhoods. Others are full destinations.

Highland Park is a 20-acre South Jordan City park that opened in 2019, built through a partnership between the city and the Daybreak HOA. It has open fields for soccer and football practice, about 10 lighted pickleball courts (placed in the middle of the park so the noise doesn't bother homeowners), a reservable pavilion, a playground, and a perimeter loop of roughly three-quarters of a mile. The view of the Wasatch Mountains alone is worth a visit.

Founders Park has Daybreak's largest community garden. Sign up for a grow box, the water is already plumbed in, and you just plant and tend. Tamara grew zucchini, tomatoes, and peas there when she first moved in, and says it was one of the easiest ways to meet her neighbors.

Downtown Daybreak

The original master plan promised a commercial center, and the developer delivered. Downtown Daybreak is home to The Ballpark at America First Square (where the Salt Lake Bees play), restaurants, a movie theater, bowling, mini golf, and a center stage for live concerts.

Tamara wasn't a baseball fan before the ballpark opened. Now she is. When the Banana Ball teams played here, players took the stage after the game for live music and autographs, and the league told Larry H. Miller organizers they wanted to come back because the atmosphere was unlike anything else in American baseball.

The Larry H. & Gail Miller Arts Center, a Salt Lake County performing arts venue, broke ground in Downtown Daybreak in 2026. Professional theater and concerts without driving downtown.

Getting Around

Daybreak was once called "too far from everything." Not anymore. The TRAX Red Line runs right into Daybreak, and many residents ride it to downtown Salt Lake City in about 30 to 40 minutes. Big-box shopping (Walmart, Costco, Target) is just outside Daybreak, and there's a walkable grocery store on North Shore by the lake.

The Daybreak Community Center is another one-of-a-kind setup. It shares a building with Daybreak Elementary. Students use the gym during the school day, and residents use it in the evenings for basketball, volleyball, the indoor track, and workout rooms.

The Cons: What You Should Know Before You Buy in Daybreak

  • Small yards. If you want a third of an acre to mow and water every weekend, Daybreak may not be for you. Tamara moved from a three-acre property and worried she'd miss it. What she found instead: massive shared parks she never has to maintain, and the option to buy a home that fronts a park. (Larger lots do exist in Daybreak if you want one.)
  • Construction noise. Daybreak is still growing, especially on the west side. You'll hear hammers and see cranes. If that bothers you, look on the east side, which is fully built out. If you like new neighbors and fresh energy, the west side might be exactly right. Just watch for nails in the road.
  • Traffic. Daybreak Parkway (11400 South) is a major artery, and traffic consistently ranks as a top concern in South Jordan resident surveys. It's a reality across the whole southwest valley.
  • The HOA. You'll need approval for exterior changes like paint colors, pergolas, and flagpoles. If you need to park a large RV beside your house, Daybreak probably isn't your fit.

The HOA: Why Tamara Changed Her Mind

Every Daybreak home (single-family, townhome, or condo) pays the master association fee. At the time of filming, it was $144.50 per month. Tamara's take: it's a pro, not a con.

That fee covers:

  • Fiber-optic internet
  • Access to all Daybreak pools
  • Free paddle board and kayak checkout
  • Access to the lake, trails, and community amenities
  • Upkeep that keeps the community in great condition

"I love it because I know they're watching everything, keeping things in great condition," she says. For the full breakdown, see our Daybreak HOA guide.

What's Coming: Central Park (Planned for 2027)

In an area of Daybreak still under construction, a new amenity called Central Park is planned to open in 2027. According to plans Tamara shared, it's expected to include a leisure pool (possibly an infinity-edge design), reservable saunas, pickleball, sand volleyball, paddle boarding, fire pits, and a dog park. If you want to live near the newest amenity in Daybreak, this is the area to watch.

The Hidden Gem: Daybreak's Tunnels

This is the one most residents don't know about, and it's the one that affects Tamara's life every day.

In Hillside Park, a series of three tunnels runs under 11400 South (Daybreak Parkway), connecting Founders Park to Eastlake Village and the lake. The developer built them at a significant cost so residents could reach Oquirrh Lake without crossing a busy road.

Tamara's son Luke once painted these tunnels for his Eagle Scout project to cover graffiti. In 2022, Daybreak commissioned an artist to fill them with big, bright murals. Today, Tamara walks through them with her grandkids, yelling "echo!" to hear it bounce back.

"My kiddos, when they were little, hopped on their bikes and rode right up to the lake and fished, and I didn't have to worry," she says. The path ends at the Reflection Pond, a small spillover pond that gives you a sneak peek of the lake before you arrive.

Daybreak Myth Busting

  • "You can't grow a vegetable garden." False. Community gardens are available, and you can grow one in your own yard. Tamara once grew a giant pumpkin.
  • "You can swim in the lake." False (now). Swimming used to be allowed, but it's prohibited today. Paddle boards, kayaks, and canoes are all welcome.
  • "Daybreak is built on toxic land." False. The land was a former Kennecott mining property, and it went through extensive soil cleanup under federal environmental oversight before any homes were built. Daybreak publishes its full environmental history. Tamara's tip: the soil is so clean you'll want to add fertilizer to your garden.
  • "You need HOA approval for exterior changes." True.
  • "Every home looks the same." False. Around six builders work in Daybreak, each with multiple floor plans and several exterior elevations per plan. There are design guidelines, but the homes are not cookie-cutter.
  • "Daybreak is too far from everything." False. Between TRAX, Downtown Daybreak, the library, and shopping, the goal is a "five-minute community" where nearly everything you need is a short walk or bike ride away.

The Bottom Line

If you want an active, outdoor lifestyle and a real sense of community, Daybreak might be a great fit. If you want full control over your paint color and landscaping, or you need RV parking, it may not be. Either way, we'll help you find the community that fits your life. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Daybreak, Utah a good place to live?

For people who value trails, parks, lake access, and walkable community amenities, Daybreak is one of the most complete master-planned communities in Utah. The main trade-offs are smaller yards, HOA design rules, and growth-related construction and traffic.

How much is the Daybreak HOA fee?

At the time this video was filmed, the Daybreak master association fee was $144.50 per month. It includes fiber-optic internet, pool access, free paddle board and kayak checkout, and access to trails and community amenities.

Is Daybreak built on contaminated land?

No. The land went through extensive soil remediation under federal environmental oversight before development began. Daybreak shares the details in its environmental history.

Can you swim in Oquirrh Lake?

No. Swimming in Oquirrh Lake is no longer allowed, but residents can paddle board, kayak, and canoe on the lake and the Watercourse.
